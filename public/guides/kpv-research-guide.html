KPV Research Guide

KPV is a tripeptide — lysine-proline-valine — matching the C-terminal fragment of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), and one of the most-searched short peptides in the research community.

What it is

KPV is the final three amino acids of α-MSH, first characterized in the 1980s–90s. The interesting finding from early work: the tiny fragment retains the anti-inflammatory activity attributed to the parent hormone in preclinical models, without engaging the full melanocortin-receptor profile — including the pigmentation signaling — associated with intact α-MSH. That separation is what made the fragment a research subject in its own right.

How it's studied

Research centers on inflammation: in vitro and animal work on NF-κB signaling and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), plus models in dermatological, gut, and respiratory contexts. It also appears in combination research — KPV is the "K" in our KLOW blend alongside GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500. It is not FDA-approved for any indication, and human data remains very limited — published findings are preclinical.

Why the interest

Three amino acids is about as simple as a peptide gets, which is exactly the double-edged sword: trivially easy to synthesize, and therefore trivially easy to mislabel. Buyer-side verification matters more for KPV than for most compounds — a COA is the only thing standing between "KPV" and "white powder."

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC with chromatogram — the short sequence should be one clean peak.
  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~342 Da (free base).
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.

Storage

Lyophilized: −20°C sealed. Reconstituted: 2–8°C. See our storage guide.
