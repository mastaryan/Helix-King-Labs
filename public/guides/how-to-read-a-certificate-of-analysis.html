How to Read a Certificate of Analysis

A COA is the lab report that proves what's in the vial. Here's what each section means — and what good numbers look like.

First: match the lot

A COA is meaningless unless it matches your vial. Check the lot code at the top of the report against the code on the vial label. Our lots start with HKL- and every COA is lookup-ready on our certificates page.

The 12-point panel, line by line

1. Identity

Usually confirmed by mass spectrometry (MS): the measured molecular weight must match the theoretical weight of the peptide sequence. Result should read "consistent" or "confirmed" — anything else is a fail.

2. Purity (HPLC)

The headline number. High-performance liquid chromatography separates the target peptide from synthesis byproducts; purity is the area of the main peak as a percentage of total. Look for ≥99%. Below 98% deserves hard questions.

3. Endotoxin

Bacterial endotoxins (LAL test) must be below the stated limit. A "pass" here matters for any cell-based work.

4. Sterility

Membrane filtration or direct inoculation testing for microbial contamination. Must read "pass" / "no growth."

5. Heavy metals

ICP-MS screening for lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury and friends, each below its limit. "Pass" across the panel.

6. Residual solvents

GC testing for leftover synthesis solvents (DMF, DCM, ether, etc.). Each must sit below its permitted limit.

7. TFA content

Trifluoroacetic acid is the counter-ion left over from HPLC purification. It's reported as a percentage — lower is better; under ~1% is typical for well-purified material.

8–12. Appearance, net content, pH behavior, water content, documentation

The supporting cast: white lyophilized powder, correct fill weight, and a complete paper trail tying the report to the lot.

Red flags

  • No named, accredited laboratory on the report.
  • Lot code on the COA doesn't match the vial.
  • Purity reported without a chromatogram.
  • Vague results ("high purity") instead of numbers.
  • A COA dated long before the lot was supposedly produced.
