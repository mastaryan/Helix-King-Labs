SS-31 (Elamipretide) Research Guide

SS-31 is a 4-amino-acid peptide that targets the mitochondria directly — and it carries something almost no other research peptide has: an FDA clearance for a mitochondrial disorder.

What it is

SS-31 (elamipretide) is a synthetic tetrapeptide (D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe) designed to concentrate in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Its defined target is cardiolipin, a lipid essential to the electron transport chain. By binding cardiolipin, SS-31 is studied for stabilizing mitochondrial structure (cristae), improving ATP production efficiency, and reducing reactive oxygen species.

Clinical history

Elamipretide has been through extensive clinical development by Stealth BioTherapeutics, including trials in heart failure, mitochondrial myopathy, and age-related macular degeneration — several of which missed primary endpoints. It has received FDA clearance for Barth syndrome, a rare genetic mitochondrial disorder — making it arguably the first mitochondria-targeted therapy to clear the FDA. That clearance covers Barth syndrome specifically; it is not blanket validation for longevity or performance use, a distinction the online hype routinely ignores.

How it's studied

Research interest clusters around mitochondrial dysfunction as a root mechanism of aging — which is why SS-31 appears constantly alongside MOTS-c and NAD+ in longevity discussions. The "mitochondrial stack" framing (SS-31 for membrane structure, MOTS-c for metabolic signaling, NAD+ for coenzyme pools) is popular online but has no human trial behind the combination.

What to look for

  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~640 Da.
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.
