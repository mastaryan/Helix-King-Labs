Tesamorelin Research Guide

Tesamorelin is a 44-amino-acid growth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH) analog — one of the few peptides in this space with an actual FDA approval behind it.

What it is

Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of human GHRH. It stimulates the pituitary to release endogenous growth hormone, which in turn raises IGF-1. Unlike direct GH secretagogues that pulse randomly, tesamorelin works through the body's own GHRH pathway, preserving the natural pulsatile pattern of GH release.

Clinical history

Tesamorelin is FDA-approved as Egrifta for the reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy — a well-documented complication of antiretroviral therapy. That approval makes it one of the very few compounds in the broader peptide-research conversation with full regulatory review behind a specific indication. Outside that indication, it remains a research compound.

How it's studied

Research interest centers on GH-axis physiology: body composition studies, visceral adipose tissue, lipid metabolism, and cognitive endpoints (early-phase trials have explored effects on cognition in older adults). Analytical work focuses on identity confirmation — as a 44-mer, it is a demanding synthesis, and truncated or misfolded copies are a known quality risk.

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC with chromatogram — long sequences punish sloppy synthesis.
  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~5135 Da.
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.

Storage

Lyophilized: −20°C sealed. Reconstituted: 2–8°C. See our storage guide.
