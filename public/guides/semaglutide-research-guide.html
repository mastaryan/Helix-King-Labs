Semaglutide Research Guide

Semaglutide is a 31-amino-acid GLP-1 receptor agonist — the compound that defined the modern metabolic-peptide era.

What it is

Semaglutide is a synthetic analog of human glucagon-like peptide-1, modified with a fatty diacid side chain that binds albumin and extends its half-life to roughly one week. It is the most clinically documented GLP-1 agonist in existence, with trial programs spanning diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular outcomes, and more.

How it's studied

Research materials are used in receptor pharmacology, analytical method development, formulation studies, and stability work. Because it is the reference compound for the entire GLP-1 class, it is also the benchmark every newer agonist is measured against — which makes verified identity and purity especially important.

Key trial landmarks

STEP program: In adults with obesity, once-weekly semaglutide produced ~15% mean weight loss at 68 weeks — the result that reset expectations for the whole field.

SELECT: In adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity, semaglutide cut major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% — the first GLP-1 trial to show a hard outcomes benefit in a non-diabetic population.

SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head: Against tirzepatide at 72 weeks, semaglutide reached 13.7% mean weight loss versus 20.2% for tirzepatide. See our tirzepatide research guide for the full comparison.

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC with chromatogram — the most counterfeited peptide on earth; demand the data.
  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~4114 Da.
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA. No shortcuts.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.
