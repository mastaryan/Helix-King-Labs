MOTS-c Research Guide

MOTS-c is a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA — one of the first "mitochondrial-derived peptides" ever described, and a fast-growing topic in metabolic research.

What it is

Discovered in 2015, MOTS-c (mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c) is a short peptide produced by the mitochondria themselves — not the nuclear genome. It acts as a signaling molecule between mitochondria and the rest of the cell, with published work linking it to AMPK pathway activation and metabolic regulation.

How it's studied

Research centers on metabolism: obesity models, insulin sensitivity, exercise-mimetic pathways, and age-related metabolic decline. Animal studies have reported effects on fat accumulation and glucose regulation, which is why it appears constantly in longevity and biohacker discussions. It is not FDA-approved for any indication, and human data remains early — most findings are preclinical.

Why the buzz

MOTS-c sits at the intersection of two hot fields — mitochondrial biology and metabolic health — which explains the outsized attention relative to the evidence base. Researchers should separate the genuine novelty (a mitochondrial-encoded signaling peptide is a real discovery) from the extrapolated claims (human longevity effects are unproven).

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC with chromatogram — short sequences are easy to make, which also makes them easy to fake.
  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~2174 Da.
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.
