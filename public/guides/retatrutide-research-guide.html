Retatrutide Research Guide

Retatrutide is a 39-amino-acid triple-agonist peptide (GIP / GLP-1 / glucagon receptors) in clinical investigation for metabolic research.

What it is

Retatrutide (LY-3437943) is a single peptide engineered to activate three receptors — GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon — making it the first triple-agonist in its class to reach late-stage clinical trials. Its 39-amino-acid sequence includes non-natural residues that extend its half-life.

How it's studied

Phase 2 trial data published in major journals report significant weight and metabolic-marker changes in trial participants. As a research material, it is studied in receptor-binding assays, stability work, and analytical comparisons. All human data comes from controlled clinical trials — it remains an investigational compound.

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC — at 39 amino acids with modified residues, synthesis is demanding; insist on the chromatogram.
  • Identity by mass spec — theoretical mass ~4731 Da.
  • Endotoxin and sterility pass — non-negotiable at this complexity.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code.

Storage

Lyophilized: −20°C sealed. This is a long, modified sequence — treat storage conservatively. See our storage guide.
