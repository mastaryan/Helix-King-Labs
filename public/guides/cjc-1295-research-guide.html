CJC-1295 Research Guide

CJC-1295 is a 29-amino-acid analog of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) — a modified GRF (1-29) engineered for stability — and one of the most referenced compounds in growth-hormone-axis research.

What it is

CJC-1295 descends from the GHRH/sermorelin lineage: the first 29 amino acids of endogenous GHRH, with four substitutions that protect it against enzymatic breakdown. In preclinical and early clinical work it was shown to stimulate the pituitary's growth-hormone pulse through the GHRH receptor — the same receptor targeted by tesamorelin, its fellow GHRH analog. It is not FDA-approved for any indication.

DAC vs no-DAC

Two forms appear in the literature, and confusing them is the most common error in this space. CJC-1295 with DAC carries a Drug Affinity Complex — a lysine-linked moiety that binds circulating albumin, extending the observed half-life to roughly a week in human studies. CJC-1295 without DAC (often labeled Mod GRF 1-29) lacks that addition and clears much faster. They are different molecules with different pharmacokinetics; a COA should state which form was tested.

How it's studied

Research focuses on the GH axis: pulsatile growth-hormone release, IGF-1 downstream signaling, and body-composition models. It frequently appears in combination research with ghrelin-receptor agonists such as ipamorelin — the rationale being complementary pathways (GHRH receptor plus ghrelin receptor) rather than overlapping ones. Published human data is limited to early-phase trials; most findings are preclinical.

Why the interest

The DAC/no-DAC split is exactly why buyer-side verification matters here: two products can both be labeled "CJC-1295" while being different compounds with different masses. Identity testing by mass spectrometry — not just a purity percentage — is what tells them apart.

What to look for

  • Purity ≥99% by HPLC with chromatogram.
  • Identity by mass spec — ~3368 Da for the no-DAC form (free base); the DAC form is substantially heavier. The COA must match the labeled form.
  • Full panel — endotoxin, sterility, metals, solvents, TFA.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab.
