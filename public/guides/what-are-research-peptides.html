What Are Research Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. "Research peptides" are those same molecules supplied strictly as laboratory materials — with paperwork to prove what they are.

The short version

Proteins are long chains of amino acids; peptides are the short ones (typically under 50 amino acids). In a research setting, peptides are studied for their biochemical properties — binding, stability, purity behavior — in controlled laboratory environments. They are research materials, not supplements, not medicines, and not for human or animal use.

How they're made

Most research peptides are built by solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS): amino acids are added one at a time to a growing chain anchored to a resin, then the finished chain is cleaved off and purified — usually by preparative HPLC. The result is a white lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder sealed in a glass vial.

Why purity matters

Synthesis isn't perfect. Side reactions create truncated or modified chains that ride along with the target molecule. A 99% pure peptide and a 95% pure peptide can behave very differently in an assay — which is why every serious vendor publishes a certificate of analysis (COA) from third-party testing. Ours cover identity, HPLC purity, endotoxin, sterility, heavy metals, residual solvents, and TFA. Learn how to read a COA.

Lyophilized vs. reconstituted

Peptides ship freeze-dried because the dry powder is far more stable than a solution. Reconstitution — dissolving the powder in bacteriostatic water — happens at the lab bench, right before the work begins. See our reconstitution guide and storage guide.

Buying responsibly

  • Demand the COA before you buy. If a vendor won't show third-party testing, walk away.
  • Check the lot code. The COA must match the lot on your vial — ours start with HKL- and are lookup-ready on our certificates page.
  • Verify the lab. A real COA names an ISO-accredited laboratory. Ours do.
