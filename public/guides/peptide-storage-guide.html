Peptide Storage Guide

Peptides degrade with heat, light, moisture, and time. Storage is simple once you know the rules.

The golden rule

Keep peptides cold, dark, dry, and sealed. Every recommendation below is a variation on that theme.

Lyophilized (freeze-dried) vials

  • Long term (months–years): −20°C freezer, original sealed vial, ideally with desiccant. This is the most stable state a peptide exists in.
  • Short term (weeks): 2–8°C refrigerator is fine for an unopened vial you'll use soon.
  • Never store at room temperature for more than brief transit periods.
  • Let a frozen vial come fully to room temperature before opening it — opening a cold vial invites condensation, and moisture is the enemy.

Reconstituted solutions

Once dissolved, the clock runs faster. Keep reconstituted peptides at 2–8°C and plan to use them within about 30 days. Do not re-freeze a solution repeatedly — freeze–thaw cycles accelerate degradation. If you must store longer, aliquot into single-use portions before the first freeze.

Light and air

UV and even strong visible light break peptide bonds over time — keep vials in their box or a dark container. Minimize air exposure: reseal promptly, and don't leave a punctured stopper sitting open on the bench.

Realistic shelf life

  • Lyophilized at −20°C, sealed: 24+ months for most sequences.
  • Lyophilized at 2–8°C, sealed: 12 months comfortably.
  • Reconstituted at 2–8°C: ~30 days.

These are conservative working numbers, not guarantees — when in doubt, fresh material and a fresh COA beat heroic storage.
