HCG Research Guide

HCG — human chorionic gonadotropin — is a 237-amino-acid glycoprotein hormone. Unlike the small synthetic peptides in most research catalogs, it's a large, fragile biologic with a very different handling and verification profile.

What it is

HCG is a heterodimer: a 92-amino-acid alpha subunit (shared with LH, FSH, and TSH) plus a 145-amino-acid beta subunit that gives it its identity — the beta subunit is what pregnancy tests detect. It acts as an LH-receptor agonist. Pharmaceutical HCG is FDA-approved for specific indications (female infertility, male hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, cryptorchidism) and is prescription-only; research-grade material is for laboratory study, not clinical use.

How it's studied

Research centers on the gonadal axis: Leydig-cell signaling, intratesticular testosterone models, and fertility-preservation protocols studied alongside testosterone replacement — which is why it appears in the same conversations as our TRT guide. Published work spans reproductive endocrinology and hypogonadism models. Human data exists but belongs to the clinical literature, not the research-chemical space.

Why it's different

Size changes everything. At ~36.7 kDa with disulfide bonds and glycosylation, HCG is roughly 25× the mass of BPC-157 — and far more fragile. It degrades with heat, agitation, and time in solution. That fragility is also why verification matters more here: a degraded biologic can still show a plausible-looking purity number while having lost its folded structure. Bioactivity assays, not just HPLC, are the meaningful check — and cold-chain handling from synthesis to vial is non-negotiable.

What to look for

  • Identity by mass spec or immunoassay — beta-subunit confirmation, not just a purity percentage.
  • Purity by HPLC/SE-HPLC — size-exclusion chromatography catches aggregates the standard reversed-phase run misses.
  • Full panel — endotoxin (critical for a biologic), sterility, host-cell residuals where applicable.
  • Lot-matched COA with your vial's HKL- code, from a named accredited lab, with a test date that fits the batch.

Storage

Lyophilized: −20°C sealed, protect from light. Reconstituted: 2–8°C and use promptly — do not shake, do not freeze-thaw. See our storage guide.
