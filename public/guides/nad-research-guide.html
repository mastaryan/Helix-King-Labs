NAD+ Research Guide

NAD+ is the biggest name in longevity research right now — ~77,000 US searches a month and climbing. Here's what the molecule actually is, and where the evidence ends and the marketing begins.

What it is

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every living cell — technically a nucleotide derivative, not a peptide, though it travels in the same research circles. It drives redox reactions that convert nutrients into cellular energy and supports DNA repair enzymes (sirtuins, PARPs). The core research premise: cellular NAD+ pools are reported to decline with age, though newer work has challenged how universal that decline is.

How it's studied

Research spans IV infusion studies, injectable NAD+, and oral precursors — primarily NR (nicotinamide riboside) and NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide), which cells convert into NAD+ through salvage pathways. Endpoints include metabolic markers, muscle function, and cognitive measures. Human data remains mixed: raising NAD+ levels has not translated into demonstrated reversal of aging in controlled trials.

The honest caveats

Three things the hype usually skips: (1) NAD+ itself crosses cell membranes poorly due to its size and charge — which is why precursor research dominates; (2) "levels decline with age" is contested in recent literature; (3) celebrity IV drips are wellness services, not clinical evidence. The molecule is genuinely central to cellular metabolism. The longevity claims are not established.
